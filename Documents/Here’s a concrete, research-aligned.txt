Here’s a concrete, research-aligned design for your **daily session builder** and **item counts**, tuned for Years 1–6, Maths/English, with spaced repetition and interleaving at the core.

## Guiding principles

- **Short, frequent sessions beat long, infrequent ones** for children’s long-term retention. [parentingscience](https://parentingscience.com/spaced-learning/)
- **Optimal session length grows with age**: roughly 15–20 min for Years 1–2, up to 25–35 min by Years 5–6. [blog.afficienta](https://blog.afficienta.com/daily-20-minute-math-apps_-how-to-build-your-childs-confidence-in-2026/)
- Each session should mix **review (spaced/retrieval)**, **new learning**, and **mixed/interleaved practice**. [teachertoolkit.co](https://www.teachertoolkit.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/s44159-022-00089-1.pdf)
- Keep cognitive load manageable: small sets per skill, varied but not overwhelming. [hiwavemakers](https://hiwavemakers.com/blog/microlearning-kids-5-minute-vs-long-sessions/)

## Recommended daily time and item counts by year level

Use these as defaults; you can let schools/parents adjust slightly within a safe band.

| Year level | Target daily time | Approx. items per session | Notes |
|------------|-------------------|---------------------------|-------|
| Years 1–2  | 15–20 minutes     | 12–18 items              | Shorter items, more visuals, more gamified feedback. |
| Years 3–4  | 20–25 minutes     | 18–24 items              | More multi-step problems; still keep pace brisk. |
| Years 5–6  | 25–35 minutes     | 24–32 items              | Can handle longer word problems and reading passages. |

These align with evidence that elementary students benefit from **15–20+ minute daily sessions**, with older children tolerating longer, deeper work. [blog.afficienta](https://blog.afficienta.com/daily-20-minute-math-apps_-how-to-build-your-childs-confidence-in-2026/)

## Session composition: review vs new vs mixed

A balanced daily session (by time, not necessarily by item count) could look like:

- **Review (spaced/retrieval):** ~40% of time  
- **New learning:** ~35% of time  
- **Mixed/interleaved practice:** ~25% of time  

This mirrors recommendations that a good session includes review of old skills, one new focus, and mixed practice combining old and new. [lumastery](https://www.lumastery.com/for-parents/how-much-math-per-day-is-enough)

Translated into **item counts** (example for Year 3, ~20 items):

- **Review items:** 8–9 items (from skills due via spaced repetition)
- **New-learning items:** 7–8 items (introducing/practising a new skill)
- **Mixed/interleaved items:** 4–5 items (combining multiple skills, including older ones)

For younger years, reduce counts but keep the proportions similar.

## Daily session builder algorithm

### Step 1: Determine session capacity

Based on year level and optionally child-specific data (attention, past completion rates):

```python
YEAR_CONFIG = {
    1: {"target_minutes": 17, "items": 14},
    2: {"target_minutes": 18, "items": 16},
    3: {"target_minutes": 22, "items": 20},
    4: {"target_minutes": 24, "items": 22},
    5: {"target_minutes": 28, "items": 26},
    6: {"target_minutes": 32, "items": 30},
}
```

You can adjust per child if they consistently finish early/late or if a school sets a custom policy.

### Step 2: Build candidate pools

From your spaced-repetition engine, build:

- **Due review skills:** all skills where `next_review_at ≤ now`.
- **Near-due review skills:** `next_review_at` within next 1–2 days (to smooth load).
- **Priority skills:** flagged as “area to give more attention”.
- **New skills to introduce:** based on curriculum sequence (VIC/NSW track) and what the class/child is currently working on.
- **Mixed-practice skills:** previously learned skills that are good candidates for interleaving (not due urgently, but beneficial to keep active).

### Step 3: Allocate item slots

For a target of `N` items (from config):

- **Review items:** `N_review = round(N * 0.40)`
- **New-learning items:** `N_new = round(N * 0.35)`
- **Mixed/interleaved items:** `N_mixed = N - N_review - N_new`

Then:

1. **Fill review slots:**
   - Prioritise:
     - Priority skills (“needs attention”).
     - Skills with lowest `stability` / lowest retrievability.
     - Then other due skills.
   - For each selected skill, pick 1–3 items depending on how many review slots remain and how weak the skill is.
   - For very weak skills, you can schedule **2–3 micro-reviews** of the same skill within the session, separated by other items. [hiwavemakers](https://hiwavemakers.com/blog/microlearning-kids-5-minute-vs-long-sessions/)

2. **Fill new-learning slots:**
   - Choose 1–2 **focus skills** to introduce/develop today (aligned to curriculum sequence).
   - For each focus skill, generate 4–6 items that:
     - Start easier, then ramp up.
     - Include worked examples or scaffolded hints early in the sequence.
   - Ensure new items are tagged clearly so you can track initial learning vs review.

3. **Fill mixed/interleaved slots:**
   - Select 3–5 previously learned skills from different strands/topics.
   - For each, pick 1–2 items that:
     - Combine concepts (e.g., addition + word problem; reading comprehension + vocabulary).
     - Are slightly varied in format (multiple-choice, short answer, drag-drop).
   - Avoid more than 2 consecutive items from the same skill/topic to maintain interleaving. 

### Step 4: Order items within the session

To maximise spacing and interleaving:

- **Start with a short warm-up:** 2–3 easy review items (high success rate) to build confidence.
- **Introduce new skill items** in a small block, but:
  - Break them up with 1–2 review items in between.
  - Example pattern for a 20-item session:
    - Items 1–3: warm-up review (different skills)
    - Items 4–8: new skill A (with a review item at position 6)
    - Items 9–11: review of priority skills
    - Items 12–16: new skill B (interleaved with 1 mixed item at position 14)
    - Items 17–20: mixed/interleaved practice across older skills
- Ensure no more than **2 consecutive items from the same skill**.

This structure supports within-session spacing and interleaving, which benefit children’s delayed recall. [hiwavemakers](https://hiwavemakers.com/blog/microlearning-kids-5-minute-vs-long-sessions/)

### Step 5: Adaptive truncation and extension

- If a child consistently finishes early:
  - Slightly increase `N` (e.g., +2 items) or add an optional “bonus challenge” section.
- If they often don’t finish:
  - Reduce `N` by 10–20% and ensure the most important review/priority skills are placed early in the session.
- Allow **session pausing** so children can resume later the same day without losing progress.

## Handling Maths vs English

The overall structure is the same, but:

- **Maths:**
  - More short, discrete items (facts, procedures, short word problems).
  - Easier to hit higher item counts in the same time.
- **English:**
  - Reading comprehension items may involve longer passages; count a passage + 3–5 questions as **one “item cluster”** in your time budgeting.
  - Writing/vocabulary tasks may take longer; adjust expected items downward accordingly.

You can maintain separate configs per subject if needed, e.g.:

- Year 3 Maths: 20 items in 22 minutes.
- Year 3 English: 14–16 item clusters in 22 minutes.

## Child-facing and parent-facing framing

- Tell parents:  
  - “Daily sessions are designed to be **15–30 minutes**, depending on year level, with a mix of review, new learning, and mixed practice.” [blog.afficienta](https://blog.afficienta.com/daily-20-minute-math-apps_-how-to-build-your-childs-confidence-in-2026/)
- Tell children:
  - “Today’s mission: warm-up, learn something new, and level up your skills.”
  - Avoid showing raw counts; use progress bars, stars, or “missions completed”.

## Example: Year 2 Maths daily session (16 items, ~18 minutes)

- **Review (≈6–7 items):**
  - 2 items: addition within 20 (priority skill, low stability).
  - 2 items: subtraction within 20 (due via spaced repetition).
  - 2–3 items: place value (tens/ones) interleaved.
- **New learning (≈6 items):**
  - Focus skill: “Adding a two-digit number and a one-digit number with regrouping”.
  - 6 items, gradually increasing difficulty, with hints and step-by-step solutions.
- **Mixed practice (≈3–4 items):**
  - 1 word problem combining addition + subtraction.
  - 1 simple geometry item (shapes).
  - 1–2 number-line items linking to earlier skills.

Ordered to interleave:

1–2: warm-up addition  
3–6: new skill (first 4 items)  
7–8: review subtraction  
9–11: new skill (remaining 2 items) + 1 mixed geometry  
12–16: mixed practice across place value, number line, word problem
