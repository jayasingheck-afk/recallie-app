## Learning design: evidence-based features

Research shows that spacing, interleaving, and retrieval practice improve long-term learning but can feel harder in the short term; good UX and gameful design help overcome this. [dl.acm](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3291279.3339411)

- **Spaced repetition:** Schedule reviews of each skill/topic across days/weeks using an adaptive algorithm (e.g., SM-2–style or half-life regression) tuned per child. [dl.acm](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3291279.3339411)
- **Interleaving:** Mix problem types within sessions (e.g., number facts + word problems + geometry) to strengthen discrimination and transfer. [dl.acm](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3291279.3339411)
- **Retrieval practice:** Low-stakes, frequent quizzes with immediate feedback; require active recall before showing solutions. [dl.acm](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3291279.3339411)
- **Gamification:** Points, streaks, level-ups, badges, and progress maps; ensure rewards reinforce effort and mastery, not just speed. [dl.acm](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3291279.3339411)

## Age-specific tone and child psychology

- Use **simple, encouraging language** for younger learners (Years 1–2), slightly more mature but still supportive tone for Years 3–6.
- Avoid negative labels: display **“Area to give more attention”** instead of “weaknesses”.
- Build in **growth-mindset messaging** (“You’re getting better with practice”), short sessions to match attention spans, and optional “brain break” prompts.

## Compliance: Australian child privacy (COPPA-like)

Your app will likely be “likely to be accessed by children”, so you must comply with the **Australian Privacy Principles (APPs)** and the **Children’s Online Privacy Code** (OAIC). [oaic.gov](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-registers/privacy-codes/childrens-online-privacy-code)

Key obligations to design for:

- **Data minimisation:** Collect only what’s strictly necessary to run the service. [oaic.gov](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/262631/Exposure-Draft-Childrens-Online-Privacy-Code.pdf)
- **Age-appropriate privacy notice:** A version of your privacy policy written for children (clear, concise, age-appropriate). [oaic.gov](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/262631/Exposure-Draft-Childrens-Online-Privacy-Code.pdf)
- **Parental controls & consent:** Mechanisms for parental consent/controls for under-13s, aligned with COPPA-like expectations and Australian practice. [au.reset](https://au.reset.tech/uploads/app-childrens-rights-briefing-paper.pdf)
- **Security:** Active measures to protect children’s data from unauthorised access, loss, or misuse (encryption, access controls, logging). [au.reset](https://au.reset.tech/uploads/app-childrens-rights-briefing-paper.pdf)
- **Privacy impact assessment:** Conduct and maintain a PIA for new services/changes affecting children’s data. [oaic.gov](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/262631/Exposure-Draft-Childrens-Online-Privacy-Code.pdf)
- **Transparency & rights:** Clear processes for complaints, access, and correction of personal information. [oaic.gov](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/read-the-australian-privacy-principles)

Practical steps:

- Default to **minimal profiling**; avoid behavioural advertising.
- Make **parental dashboards** the place for detailed data; keep child views simple.
- Document your data flows and run a formal PIA before launch. [oaic.gov](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/262631/Exposure-Draft-Childrens-Online-Privacy-Code.pdf)
